15 · Two measures over time

Never use two y-axes

With two axes, the scale of each is arbitrary. Change one and the lines track, separate or cross at will.

On the right: A and B are unrelated. Only the right axis changes between frames.

Put both on one axis as an index: A grew 169%, B 25%.

Plot Sense · 21

A dual-axis chart puts two measures with different units on the same plot, one scaled to the left axis and one to the right. The problem is that the alignment between the two scales is arbitrary, and the eye reads crossings and parallel movement as meaningful. In the animation, series A is a steadily rising measure and series B is a random walk with a slight upward drift, generated independently of A. Only the range of the right axis changes from frame to frame. Fit the right axis to B and B appears to track A, because both happen to trend up. Widen it and B looks flat. Widen it the other way and A seems to race past a flat B. None of these is a property of the data. The honest alternative is to index both series to 100 at the start and draw them on a single axis: A grew 169 percent while B grew 25 percent. Or use two separate charts stacked with the same time axis.
