02 · How the eye reads

Position beats length beats angle beats color

1 · Position on a shared axis: most precise

2 · Length bars from a common baseline

3 · Angle pie slices, slopes

4 · Area bubbles, treemaps

5 · Color shade or intensity: least precise

Eye rule: precise comparison → position

Use color and area for a rough pattern, never for a close call.

Plot Sense · 04　　Ranking from Cleveland & McGill, “Graphical Perception” (1984).

Cleveland and McGill's 1984 experiments ranked how accurately people judge quantities from different visual encodings. Position along a common scale was most accurate, then length, then angle and slope, then area, with color shade near the bottom. The animation lets you feel it. The same five values, all between 19 and 27, are drawn as dots at a height, as bars, as pie slices, as circle areas and as color shades. In the top two rows the largest value is obvious. In the pie, the circles and the color squares you have to guess. That is why a bar chart beats a pie chart for close values, and why a heatmap is good for spotting a pattern but bad for reading an exact number.
