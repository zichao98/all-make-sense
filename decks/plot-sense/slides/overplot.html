11 · Too many points

With big data, plot density

20,000 dots overlap into a solid blob. The plot shows where data exist, not how much is there.

Transparency: overlaps get darker

2D histogram / hexbin: count per cell, shown as shade

Sample: plot a random subset

Eye rule: past a few thousand points

Switch from dots to density, or you will miss where the data really are.

Plot Sense · 16　　Simulated data. The 30% share is the mixing weight used to generate it.

Scatter plots break down when there are many points, because dots pile on top of each other and the plot turns solid. The animation draws 20,000 points: about 70 percent spread widely and about 30 percent packed into one small cluster. With solid dots, the cluster is invisible; the plot only tells you where data exist. With 5 percent opacity, overlapping regions grow darker and the cluster appears. The 2D histogram counts points per cell and maps the count to a single-hue shade, which is the most direct answer to 'where is most of the data?'. Hexagonal bins do the same with a nicer tiling. Random subsampling also works if you only need the overall shape.
