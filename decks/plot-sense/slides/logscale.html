06 · Skew and the log axis

Skewed data needs a log axis

Response times, incomes, file sizes: most values are small, a few are huge. On a linear axis everything piles into one spike.

Median: 98 ms

Mean: 165 ms, pulled up by the tail

99th percentile: 1,150 ms

Eye rule: max ≫ median? Try a log axis

Report the median and a high percentile (p95, p99), not just the mean.

Plot Sense · 09　　Simulated log-normal data. The “max ≫ median” test is a rule of thumb.

Many real quantities are right-skewed: most values are modest and a few are enormous. The animation uses 1,000 simulated response times from a log-normal distribution with a median around 100 ms. On a linear axis almost everything sits in the first few bars and the tail, up to 2,681 ms, runs off the chart. On a log axis, where each step is a factor of 10, the same data become a symmetric bell and you can read the spread. Skew also separates the mean from the median: here the median is 98 ms but the mean is 165 ms, because a few slow requests pull it up. The 99th percentile is 1,150 ms. For skewed data, the median and high percentiles describe the experience better than the mean. Treat the max-versus-median test as a rule of thumb.
