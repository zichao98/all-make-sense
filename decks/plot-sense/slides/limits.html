Where these rules bend

Rules of thumb, not laws

√n bins, 1.5 × IQR whiskers and “pie for 2–3 parts” are conventions. Break them when you can say why.

Software defaults decide for you

Automatic axes, bin counts and color scales are choices someone else made. Check each one.

Every chart is an argument

What you highlight, sort and smooth steers the reader. Make the choice deliberately and say what you did.

Color is not seen equally

Some readers cannot tell red from green. Carry meaning with position and labels, not color alone.

Four caveats. First, most of the rules here are conventions with good reasons, not mathematical facts: the square-root bin rule, Tukey's 1.5 IQR whiskers, the advice to limit pies to two or three parts. Break them when you can explain why. Second, plotting software makes many of these choices silently, including truncated axes, default bin counts and rainbow color maps, so check the defaults. Third, every chart is an argument: what you sort, highlight, smooth and leave out steers the reader, so make those choices on purpose and disclose them. Fourth, color perception varies; a noticeable share of people have red-green color vision deficiency. The safest charts carry their message in position and labels, with color as reinforcement.
