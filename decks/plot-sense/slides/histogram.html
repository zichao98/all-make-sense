A real feature survives every reasonable choice. Noise does not.

Plot Sense · 07

A histogram splits the range into equal bins and draws one bar per bin, with height proportional to the count. The bin count is a decision, and the animation shows why it matters. The data are 500 values from two overlapping groups. With 3 bins the two humps merge and the data look like one lump. Around 8 bins the two humps are clear. With 22 bins, the square-root rule, you see the shape plus some detail. With 80 bins most of what you see is noise. Two common starting points disagree for these data: the square root of 500 gives 22 bins, and the Freedman–Diaconis width, twice the interquartile range times n to the minus one third, is 0.86, about 9 bins over this range. Neither is the answer. Try a few and trust only the features that survive.
