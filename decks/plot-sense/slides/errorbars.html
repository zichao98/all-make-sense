09 · Comparing averages

A mean without spread is half the story

Both panels compare 20 values in A with 20 in B. The means are 10.0 and 11.0 in each.

95% interval for a mean. Here ±1.40 when SD = 3, ±0.23 when SD = 0.5.

Eye rule: show points or an interval

And say which one: SD, standard error and 95% CI are very different widths.

Plot Sense · 13

A bar chart of two means hides the question that matters: is the difference larger than the noise? Both panels show groups A and B with 20 values each and means of exactly 10.0 and 11.0, so their bar charts are identical. In the left panel the values have a standard deviation of 3. The 95 percent confidence interval for each mean is the mean plus or minus t times s over the square root of n, with t = 2.093 for 19 degrees of freedom, which is ±1.40. The two intervals overlap heavily, so a one-point difference could easily be noise. In the right panel the standard deviation is 0.5, the interval is ±0.23, and the groups are clearly apart. Showing the points, or at least an interval, is what turns a pair of bars into evidence. Always label which interval you drew.
