05 · Box plot

A box plot can hide the shape

Middle line: the median

Box: the middle 50%, Q1 to Q3 (the IQR)

Whiskers: the furthest points within 1.5 IQR

Circles: points beyond the whiskers

The trap: the three datasets on the right have the same median and quartiles. One is a bell, one is two clusters, one is flat.

Plot Sense · 08　　Whisker and outlier rule: Tukey's 1.5 × IQR convention.

A box plot is a five-number summary: the median, the first and third quartiles that bound the box, and whiskers that reach the furthest data points within 1.5 interquartile ranges of the box. Anything beyond is drawn as an individual point. It is compact and great for comparing many groups side by side. But it cannot show shape. The animation cycles through three datasets of 150 values that were transformed to have the same median, 0, and the same quartiles, −0.675 and 0.675. One is bell-shaped, one is two separate clusters with almost nothing in the middle, and one is flat. Their boxes are essentially identical, while the dots below show three different distributions. The 1.5 IQR whisker rule is a convention from John Tukey, not a law.
