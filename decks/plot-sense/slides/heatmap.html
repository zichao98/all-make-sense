12 · Heatmaps and color

Color has a job: match it to the data

Sequential: one hue, light → dark, for amounts

Diverging: two hues around a neutral midpoint, for ± changes

Rainbow: avoid; hue jumps invent edges

A heatmap uses position for two categories and color for the value. Good for patterns, weak for exact numbers.

Eye rule: amount → one hue. Change → two hues

If exact values matter, print the numbers in the cells.

Plot Sense · 17

A heatmap uses two positions, here weekday and hour, and encodes the value as color. Since color is the least precise channel, heatmaps are for spotting patterns, not reading exact values. The color scale must match the job of the data. For an amount, use one hue from light to dark, so darker always means more: the weekday 9:00 peak and the daily 20:00 peak stand out. A rainbow scale looks colorful but creates sharp visual boundaries wherever the hue changes, from green to yellow for example, even though the data change smoothly. For a change that can be positive or negative, use two hues that read as opposites with a pale neutral at zero, so the midpoint means nothing happened. The data here are synthetic.
