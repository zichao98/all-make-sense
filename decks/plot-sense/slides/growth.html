14 · Growth and the log axis

On a log axis, steady growth is a straight line

On a linear axis every growing series curves upward. You cannot tell steady growth from slowing growth.

On a log axis equal steps are equal ratios, so a constant growth rate is a straight line and slowing growth bends down.

Doubling time at growth g per period: 14.2 periods at 5% (rule of 72: 14.4).

Plot Sense · 20

Exponential growth fools linear axes. Both series in the animation start at 100. Series A grows a steady 5 percent per period and ends at 1,779 after 59 periods. Series B starts faster, at 8.5 percent, but its growth rate shrinks by 1.8 percent of itself each period, down to 2.9 percent at the end, so it is steadily slowing. It still ends higher, at 2,048. On a linear axis both simply curve upward, and B even looks like the winner. On a log axis each grid step is a multiplication, so a constant growth rate becomes a straight line, and the slope is the rate. Series A is straight; series B visibly bends. The doubling time at growth rate g is ln 2 divided by ln(1 + g), 14.2 periods at 5 percent, close to the rule-of-72 estimate of 72 / 5 = 14.4.
