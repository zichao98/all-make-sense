07 · Logarithms

Logs: ×10 in, +1 out. They count steps

Log asks: how many ×10 steps does it take to get from 1 to x?

1,000 → 3

three zeros

1,000,000 → 6

six zeros

2 → 0.30

not even one full step

Base 2

1 billion → about 30: halve it 30 times to reach 1

Logs squeeze big numbers: going from 100 to 1,000 moves the log only from 2 to 3.

Function Sense · 10

A logarithm is the reverse fingerprint of an exponential: it turns multiplying into adding. Multiply x by 10 and log base 10 of x goes up by exactly 1. So log base 10 roughly counts digits: 1,000 has log 3, a million has log 6. With base 2 it counts halvings or doublings: log base 2 of a billion is about 29.9, so you can halve a billion about 30 times before reaching 1. That is why binary search through a billion sorted items takes about 30 steps. In the animation the top ruler is ordinary x from 0 to 1000, the bottom is log x. The blue stretch from 1 to 10 is tiny on top but gets a full unit below. The orange stretch from 100 to 1000 is huge on top but also gets just one unit. As the black dot moves at constant speed on top, the log dot races at first and then crawls.
