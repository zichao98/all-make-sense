18 · Matrix multiplication

Multiplying matrices = chaining maps

BA means: do A first, then B. Again read right to left.

Areas get scaled twice, so the area factors multiply.

In 1D: stretch by c, then by a, is one stretch by ac.

In general AB ≠ BA: shear-then-rotate and rotate-then-shear land in different places.

Function Sense · 23

Because a matrix is a function on vectors, chaining two matrices is just composition, and the matrix of the chain is the product. BA times x equals B applied to A x, so BA means do A first, then B. The animation applies a shear A and then a 90 degree rotation B, and then does it the other way round. The results differ: BA has columns (0, 1) and (−1, 1), while AB has columns (1, 1) and (−1, 0). The dashed outline shows where the unit square would have ended up with the other order. That is why matrix multiplication is not commutative. Determinants multiply because area gets scaled twice; here both are 1, so the area of the square stays 1 in both orders. In one dimension all of this collapses to ordinary multiplication: stretching by c and then by a is one stretch by ac.
