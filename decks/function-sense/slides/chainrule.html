19 · The chain rule

Chain two machines: the stretches multiply

Zoom in and each machine is just a stretch. Run two in a row and the stretches multiply.

Near 1.2, squaring stretches by 2.4. Near 1.44, squaring again stretches by 2.88.

In many dimensions each stretch is a Jacobian matrix, and they multiply as matrices. Backpropagation in neural networks is exactly this.

Function Sense · 24

This combines the zoom idea from Part Three with composition. Up close, every smooth machine is a stretch by its derivative. If you run two machines in a row, a tiny interval gets stretched by the first factor and then by the second, so the total stretch is the product. That is the chain rule. The animation uses x squared followed by squaring again, which is x to the 4, around x = 1.2. A small interval of width h around 1.2 becomes 2.4 times wider after the first squaring, because the slope of x squared at 1.2 is 2.4. It lands around 1.44, where the second squaring stretches by 2 × 1.44 = 2.88. The product is 6.912, exactly the derivative of x to the 4 at 1.2, which is 4 × 1.2 cubed. The measured ratios on screen approach these values as the interval shrinks. In several dimensions each local stretch is a Jacobian matrix and the chain rule multiplies them, and backpropagation in neural networks is this chain rule applied layer by layer.
