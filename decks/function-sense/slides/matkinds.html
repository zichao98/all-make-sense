23 · Matrices

For matrices, the shape tells you the kind

det ≠ 0: both

The grid is tilted and stretched but stays a full grid. Undo with A⁻¹.

det = 0: neither

The plane is squashed onto a line: points collide, and everything off the line is missed.

Tall: one-to-one

The plane sits flat inside space. No collisions, but most of space is missed.

Wide: onto

A shadow on the floor. Every spot gets a shadow, but a whole vertical line shares one.

Dimension rule: you can’t be one-to-one into a smaller space, or onto a bigger one

For square matrices the two questions collapse into one: one-to-one, onto and det ≠ 0 are the same thing.

Function Sense · 29

Linear maps make the two questions especially easy, because the answer depends mostly on the shape of the matrix. A square 2 by 2 matrix with nonzero determinant is a bijection: the grid stays a full grid. With determinant 0 it squashes the plane onto a line or a point, so it is neither. A tall 3 by 2 matrix takes the plane into three-dimensional space; at best it lays the plane flat inside space, which is one-to-one but leaves most of space missed. A wide 2 by 3 matrix takes space down to the plane, like a shadow on the floor; at best every spot on the floor gets a shadow, which is onto, but all the points on one vertical line share the same shadow. That is the dimension rule: you cannot squeeze a bigger space one-to-one into a smaller one, or cover a bigger space with a smaller one. For square matrices, one-to-one, onto, and nonzero determinant are all equivalent.
