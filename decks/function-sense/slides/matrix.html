12 · Matrices

A matrix moves every point of the plane

A matrix is a function whose inputs and outputs are points (vectors). Apply it to every point and the whole grid moves.

The two columns are where the two unit arrows land. Orange = first column, blue = second. Know those, and you know where every point goes.

= how much areas get multiplied

Linear means: grid lines stay straight, parallel and evenly spaced, and the origin stays put.

Function Sense · 16

Here is a matrix acting as a function. Every point of the plane is an input; the matrix tells you where it goes. Because the map is linear, you only need to know where the two unit arrows land, and those are exactly the two columns of the matrix. Every other point is a combination of the two arrows, so it lands at the same combination of the new arrows. The animation cycles through a stretch, a shear, a 45 degree rotation, a flip, and a collapse. The shaded square shows how areas change: its area is the determinant. A flip has determinant −1, meaning the orientation reverses. The collapse has determinant 0: the whole plane is squashed onto a line, and you can't undo that. Compare with one dimension: f(x) = ax multiplies lengths by the size of a, and flips when a is negative.
