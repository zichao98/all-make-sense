15 · The bridge

Zoom in: every smooth curve is a line

Near any point, a smooth function acts like a straight line. Its slope there, f′(x₀), is the local stretch factor.

Near 1, x² is a line with slope 2 plus a tiny extra h². At h = 0.01 that extra is 0.0001.

In 2D the slope becomes a matrix J. Calculus is linear algebra, one tiny patch at a time.

Function Sense · 19

This is the bridge between everything so far. Zoom into any smooth curve and it straightens out into a line. Near x₀, f(x₀ + h) is about f(x₀) plus the slope times h, where the slope is the derivative f′(x₀). For x squared near 1, the exact expansion is 1 + 2h + h². The 1 + 2h part is the tangent line, and h² is the error. The animation zooms from a window of ±2 down to ±0.01. The black bar is the gap between curve and line at the edge of the window. In absolute terms it shrinks like h squared; relative to the picture it shrinks like h, so at ×200 zoom the gap is about 0.2 percent of the picture height and invisible. So the derivative is just the a of the ax + b that the function looks like up close. In two or more dimensions, that a becomes a matrix, the Jacobian, which ties calculus back to linear algebra.
