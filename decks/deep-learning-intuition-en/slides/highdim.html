So why does deep learning work on images?

Data occupies a tiny slice

Cat photos fill only a tiny part of pixel space. Their effective dimension is much lower.

Functions have layers too

Pixels → edges → shapes → objects. Depth is good at this kind of composition.

A smooth enough spectrum

Barron's bound is independent of dimension. Limited high-frequency content keeps the task manageable.

Deep Learning Intuition · 25 · 1D formulas build intuition. Real network sizes still need experiments.

The earlier formulas assume a 1D input. Tiling d-dimensional space with small piecewise linear regions takes roughly (1/√ε)^d cells: the curse of dimensionality. Deep learning succeeds when the data and target function have structure, such as a low-dimensional manifold, composition, or a smooth spectrum. That's also why depth, which supports composition, can matter more than width.
