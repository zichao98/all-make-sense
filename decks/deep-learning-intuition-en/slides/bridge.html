Neurons ≈ important plane waves × kinks per wave

That's the idea behind Barron's proof: even in many dimensions, few important waves can mean few neurons. A noise-like function with every direction and frequency is expensive for any method.

Deep Learning Intuition · 23

This connects the whole story. Split a high-dimensional function into Fourier plane waves. Each wave varies along just one direction k, so projecting to t = k·x turns it into a 1D wave. Trace that wave with ReLU segments: all neurons have w equal to k, with different biases placing their kinks at different locations. To estimate neurons in high dimensions, ask: how many important directions and frequencies are there, and how many kinks does each need? Barron's 1993 proof followed a related route: plane waves to 1D sigmoid steps, then random sampling, yielding an error bound independent of dimension.
