Barron's result uses sigmoid-like activations; related versions exist for ReLU. The key direction is the same: more high-frequency content means more wiggles and more neurons. This is another view of the curvature budget. The second derivative of sin(kπx) carries a factor k²π²; taking its square root makes the budget proportional to k. So 5 times the frequency needs 5 times the neurons. Barron's error is mean squared error, and the bound is independent of dimension: one theoretical reason networks can handle high-dimensional data.
