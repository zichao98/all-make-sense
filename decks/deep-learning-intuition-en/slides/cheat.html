Take this with you

One neuron = one kink

Each ReLU bends at one point: b sets position, w sets slope.

One layer = straight segments

N neurons give at most N kinks. Counting neurons means counting segments.

B is the interval's curvature budget. Precision ×10 means neurons ×3.2.

Eyeball it

For 1% error: 6–10 neurons per bend, about 17 per wave. For training, allow ×2–5.

Depth multiplies segments

L layers can give 2ᴸ segments. Higher precision makes depth's savings bigger.

Fourier = fixed sin neurons

A network learns its own basis. Training usually learns low frequencies before high ones.

Back to the first question: how many neurons for a fifth-degree function? Start with its shape. x⁵ on [−1, 1] bends mainly near the ends. For 1% error, 13 neurons suffice in theory; allowing 30–60 in actual training is safer. A fifth-degree polynomial with four swings, like T₅, needs 41 in theory. Want to use fewer? Add depth.
