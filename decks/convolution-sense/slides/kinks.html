Convolution Sense · 05

Here is the bridge from kinks to kernels. The blue line is a one-layer ReLU function with 4 kinks: at 4 the slope rises by 0.6, at 9 it drops by 1.1, at 13.5 it rises by 0.9, and at 17 it drops by 0.7. We sample it at every integer, so the spacing is h = 1, and slide the kernel (1, −2, 1) across the samples. This kernel is the discrete second derivative. If the three samples lie on one straight line, the middle one is the average of its neighbours, so the result is exactly 0. If a kink sits on the middle sample, the result equals the slope jump times h. So the output is zero almost everywhere, with a spike at each kink: 0.6, −1.1, −0.7. The kink at 13.5 is not on a sample. Its 0.9 is shared between the two neighbouring outputs, 0.45 each, and the total is still right. The spike positions are the neurons' kink locations, and the spike heights are their output weights. One kernel reads the network back. In two dimensions the same idea, a Laplacian kernel, lights up crease lines.
