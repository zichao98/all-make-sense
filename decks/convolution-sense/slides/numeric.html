Kernel w

9 weights, used at every position.

At each spot: multiply the 9 overlapping pairs and add them up.

Positions per axis: 5 − 3 + 1 = 3, so the output is 3×3.

Input, kernel and outputs are the numerical example in Dumoulin & Visin (2016), “A guide to convolution arithmetic for deep learning”.

Convolution Sense · 08

This is the example from Figure 1.1 of Dumoulin and Visin's guide, with the same numbers. The 5×5 input is blue; the 3×3 kernel with weights 0 1 2 / 2 2 0 / 0 1 2 is the shaded window, and its weights are the small orange digits. At the first position the 9 products add up to 12. The window then moves one step right, giving 12 again and then 17, and so on down the rows. The full output is 12 12 17 / 10 17 19 / 9 6 14. I recomputed all nine values to check them. Along each axis the window fits in 5 − 3 + 1 = 3 places, so the output is 3×3. Apply a bias and ReLU and each output is one neuron. All 9 of them share the same 9 weights.
