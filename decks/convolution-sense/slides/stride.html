Stride s = slide by 1, then keep every s-th output: built-in subsampling.

Inputs 5 and 6 both give 3 outputs. That ambiguity returns for transposed convolutions.

Convolution Sense · 15

Stride s means the kernel jumps s cells instead of 1. Counting positions again: from the left edge the kernel can take the floor of (i + 2p − k)/s steps before it would run past the right edge, plus the starting position. That gives o = floor((i + 2p − k)/s) + 1, the general rule in the guide; every earlier formula is a special case. The floor matters. With i = 5, k = 3, s = 2, p = 1, the padded input is 7 wide and the kernel fits at 0, 2 and 4: 3 outputs. With i = 6 the padded input is 8 wide, but a fourth jump would overhang, so the output is still 3, and the last padded row and column are never covered. They are gray in the animation. The guide notes that stride can also be read as subsampling: compute every stride-1 output, then keep one in s. Because inputs 5 and 6 map to the same output size, going backwards is ambiguous. That will matter for transposed convolutions.
