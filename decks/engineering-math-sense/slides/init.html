Engineering Math Sense · 20　　Scale = standard deviation of pre-activations; input has unit variance. Table: theory; plot: one random draw.

Here is the gain from the previous slide, set by a single number. Initialize each weight with variance g² divided by the number of inputs. A ReLU passes about half of its inputs and zeroes the rest, so each layer multiplies the variance of the signal by g²/2, and the standard deviation by the square root of that: 0.71 for g² = 1, 1.00 for g² = 2, 1.22 for g² = 3. Over 20 layers that compounds. Starting from unit-variance input, the first layer's pre-activations have standard deviation √g², and then nineteen more factors follow. For g² = 1 the scale at layer 20 is about 0.0014, for g² = 2 it stays at 1.41, and for g² = 3 it reaches about 81. The animation actually runs a 20-layer ReLU network of width 256 on eight random inputs in your browser and plots the measured standard deviation per layer. This draw gives 0.00138, 1.41 and 81.2, matching the theory. g² = 2 is the He initialization from He, Zhang, Ren and Sun's 2015 paper on rectifiers.
