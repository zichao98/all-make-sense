Engineering Math Sense · 21

The fifth row of the symptom map: great on training data, worse in production. Take 10 points from a sine wave plus Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.2, and fit polynomials by least squares. Degree 1 is too simple: training and test errors are both high, 0.42 and 0.46. Degree 3 is about right: 0.15 train and 0.24 test, close to the floor of 0.2 set by the noise itself. Degree 9 has 10 coefficients for 10 points, so it passes through every training point and the training error is zero. Between the points it swings wildly, and the median test error is 2.31, ten times worse than degree 3. These are medians over 2,000 random datasets; the animation shows one dataset close to the median: 0.41, 0.15 and 0.00 train, 0.46, 0.23 and 2.32 test. When training error is far below test error, the optimizer has done its job too well. The fix lives in statistics: more data, fewer effective parameters, regularization, or a split that reflects the deployment distribution.
