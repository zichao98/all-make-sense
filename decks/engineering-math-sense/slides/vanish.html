Eye rule: watch the gain, not the depth

Keep it near 1 with initialization, normalization and residual paths. A sigmoid’s slope is at most 0.25: ten layers can cost 10⁶.

Engineering Math Sense · 19　　Idealized: every layer scales the gradient by the same γ. Thumbnails are on a log scale; dashed line = 1.

Backpropagation multiplies one factor per layer. If each layer scales the gradient's size by a gain γ, then after L layers the gradient has been scaled by γ to the power L. Small differences compound: 0.9 to the 50th is 0.0052, so the gradient reaching the first layer is two hundred times smaller than at the top. 1.1 to the 50th is 117. The ratio between those two cases is about 22,800, from a 10 percent difference per layer. The sigmoid makes this worse because its derivative is at most 0.25, so ten sigmoid layers can multiply a gradient by 0.25 to the 10th, about 9.5 × 10⁻⁷. In a real network the per-layer factor is a Jacobian matrix, and its singular values play the role of γ; that is the 'weight spectrum' in the symptom map. The cures all keep the gain near 1: initialization scaled to the layer width, normalization layers, and residual connections that add an identity path.
