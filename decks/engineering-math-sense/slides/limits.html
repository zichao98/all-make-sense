Where this map bends

One symptom, many causes

A NaN can come from a zero inside a log in your data, not only from overflow. The map picks the first check, not the verdict.

Bowl rules are local

The step-size limit is exact only for a quadratic. Real curvature changes during training, so the limit moves with it.

Adam changes the rules

Adaptive optimizers rescale each direction by its recent gradient size, so plain gradient-descent rules hold only roughly.

Toy sizes, real directions

The overfitting and initialization numbers use small models and fixed seeds. Your values will differ; the trends will not.

Four honest caveats. First, the symptom map is a starting point. A NaN often comes from the data, a log of zero, a division by an empty count, a corrupted sample, rather than from the model. Use the map to choose the first check, then verify. Second, the step-size rule η < 2/λmax is exact for a quadratic bowl. Real losses are only locally quadratic and their curvature changes during training, which is one reason learning-rate warm-up and schedules exist. Third, Adam and similar optimizers divide each coordinate's step by a running estimate of its gradient size, so they do not obey the plain gradient-descent analysis exactly, although step size versus curvature still matters. Fourth, the experiments here are deliberately small: a 20-layer network of width 256 and polynomial fits to 10 points. The specific numbers will change for your model; the direction of each effect will not.
