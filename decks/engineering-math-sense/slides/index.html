Engineering Math Sense · 18　　Table adapted from the Capstone Lab article.

This is the article's debugging index, and it is the practical payoff of the whole deck. Each row starts from something you observe and points to the math behind it. An oscillating or non-converging loss is usually about step size versus curvature, which is calculus, optimization and numerical analysis. Vanishing or exploding gradients come from multiplying many layer Jacobians, so check activations, initialization, normalization and the weight spectrum. NaNs and run-to-run swings are numerical or statistical: overflow, precision, conditioning, sampling variance, seeds. Slowness and memory errors are linear algebra and number formats. A model that is great in training and worse in production is a statistics problem: overfitting, distribution shift, biased samples, the data split. Unstable time-series models are dynamical systems. Noisy signals and expensive convolutions are Fourier problems. The next four slides work through rows two, four and five with numbers; row three was the subject of the numerics slide.
