Eye rule: read the shape first

(batch, height, width, channels) tells you the memory bill before anything runs.

Engineering Math Sense · 07　　MB = 10⁶ bytes. 768 is a common embedding width; other models use other sizes.

Once data is discretized it is arranged as arrays. An RGB image of 224 by 224 pixels is 150,528 numbers. A second of 16 kHz audio is 16,000. A sentence cut into 512 tokens, each represented by a 768-dimensional embedding, is 393,216 numbers. Relations become arrays too: a graph becomes an adjacency matrix, a ratings table becomes a user-by-item matrix. Training works on batches, so the leading dimension multiplies everything: 32 images are 4,816,896 numbers, which is 19,267,584 bytes, about 19.3 MB, in 32-bit floats. Reading the shape of a tensor is the quickest estimate of memory and compute you can make.
