01 · The training loop

Four lines of code, four branches of math

Every training step runs the same four lines. Each is a chapter of the engineering-math syllabus at work.

Linear algebra: arrange and transform data

Probability: score the gap to the truth

Calculus: find the direction to move

Optimization: take the step, then repeat

Subjects you studied apart run together

On the right, a real model y = xW + b fits 24 noisy points. Watch the highlighted line: that is the math running now.

Engineering Math Sense · 02

The article opens with the four lines that appear in almost every deep learning script. y = x @ W + b is linear algebra: it arranges the inputs as a matrix and transforms them. loss = criterion(y, t) is probability and statistics: it scores how far the prediction is from the target. Here the criterion is mean squared error, which is the negative log-likelihood of Gaussian noise up to constants. loss.backward() is calculus: the chain rule gives the slope of the loss with respect to every parameter. optimizer.step() is numerical optimization: move a little against the slope and repeat. The animation runs exactly these four lines on 24 points generated from 1.5x + 0.5 plus noise. The first three steps are slow so you can see each line act: the line is drawn, the residuals appear, the gradient is computed, and the line moves. Then it speeds up. With learning rate 0.25 the loss falls from 2.70 to 0.045 in 24 steps, and W and b settle at 1.43 and 0.51, close to the true 1.5 and 0.5. The rest is noise.
