One-page takeaway

Four lines, four branches

Forward pass = linear algebra, loss = probability, backward = calculus, step = optimization.

Three translations

Discretize, arrange as arrays, iterate. Sample at least twice per wiggle.

Matrices stretch

Singular values are the stretch factors. Their ratio κ costs about log₁₀ κ digits.

The steepest direction caps the step; the flattest sets how long it takes.

Start near ln K

Cross-entropy is surprise. An untrained 10-class model should start near 2.30.

Keep the gain near 1

Over 50 layers, a gain of 0.9 leaves 0.005 and 1.1 gives 117. For ReLU, use g² = 2.

Back to the article's question: what is the relationship between advanced engineering mathematics and deep learning? The four lines of a training step are four branches of math running together: linear algebra arranges and transforms data, probability scores the prediction, calculus finds the direction to move, optimization takes the step. Before any of that, reality is discretized, arranged as arrays and solved by iteration. Each branch leaves a fingerprint you can recognize. Matrices stretch space and their condition number eats digits. Floats overflow, so softmax subtracts the max. The steepest curvature caps the learning rate at 2/λmax, and gradient descent is Euler's method with the same stability rule. Cross-entropy starts near ln K. Gains per layer compound over depth, so initialization and normalization keep them near 1. Training error far below test error is a statistics problem. The practical skill the article asks for is this reflex: see a symptom, name the math, check the right thing first.
