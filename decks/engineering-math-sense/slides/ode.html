Engineering Math Sense · 15　　Gradient descent is Euler’s method applied to the gradient flow of the loss.

Euler's method solves dx/dt = f(x) by stepping x forward by h times the current velocity. Put f = −∇L and you get gradient descent with h playing the role of η. So the stability rule from the last slide is the classical Euler stability rule: on dx/dt = −λx, Euler multiplies x by 1 − hλ each step and is stable only if h < 2/λ. A residual block computes x + F(x), which has the same shape as one Euler step, so a deep ResNet can be read as a discretized flow. Neural ODEs take that idea literally and let an ODE solver choose the steps. Diffusion models generate samples by integrating a learned differential equation in many small denoising steps. Using fewer steps means a larger step size, and each step makes more error, which is why fast samplers need better integrators. When a time-series model is unstable, the article suggests looking at exactly these things: the state update, the step size and the stability of the dynamics.
