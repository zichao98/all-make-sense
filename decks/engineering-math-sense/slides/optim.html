The steepest direction caps the step size

On a bowl with curvature λ, each step multiplies the error by 1 − ηλ.

Every direction must shrink. The steepest curvature, λmax, sets the limit.

The flattest direction sets the time. At κ = 100, a 100× cut takes about 460 steps.

Bouncing or blowing up? η is near 2/λmax.

Steady but slow? The problem is κ. Rescale directions (normalization, Adam) instead of pushing η.

Engineering Math Sense · 14　　Exact for a quadratic bowl; real losses are only locally quadratic.

Near a minimum, a loss looks like a bowl. Along a direction with curvature λ, one gradient step multiplies the error by 1 − ηλ. If that factor has magnitude below 1 the error shrinks; above 1 it grows. Since every direction has to shrink, the learning rate must satisfy η < 2/λmax. The animation uses curvatures 1 and 10, so the limit is 0.2. At η = 0.05 the path is smooth but crawls along the flat direction, loss 0.16 after 30 steps. At η = 0.18 the steep direction overshoots and bounces with factor −0.8, yet everything converges, loss about 3 × 10⁻⁵. At η = 0.21 the factor is −1.1 and each bounce grows: loss about 1,200 after 30 steps. The flat direction sets the time. At η = 1/λmax it shrinks by 1 − 1/κ per step, so with κ = 100 a 100-fold reduction takes ln 100 / −ln 0.99 ≈ 458 steps. Ill-conditioning is fixed by changing the geometry: normalization layers and adaptive optimizers effectively rescale directions.
