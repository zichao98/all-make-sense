06 · Linear algebra

A matrix stretches space along two axes

One layer: multiply by W, then shift by b.

Every matrix: rotate, stretch along two axes by σ₁ and σ₂, rotate.

How unevenly W stretches. Near 1 is healthy; large means one direction is almost crushed.

Rule of thumb: you lose about log₁₀ κ digits

float32 carries about 7 digits. With κ = 10⁴, expect about 3 reliable digits when you solve with W.

Engineering Math Sense · 10

A layer computes y = Wx + b. The singular value decomposition says any matrix acts in three moves: rotate, stretch along two perpendicular axes by the singular values σ₁ and σ₂, and rotate again. The animation shows a unit circle becoming an ellipse whose semi-axes are exactly σ₁ and σ₂. For the first matrix they are 1.85 and 0.60, so the condition number κ = σ₁/σ₂ is 3.1. The second matrix has nearly parallel columns: σ₁ = 2.06 and σ₂ = 0.040, so κ is about 52 and the circle collapses toward a line. A direction that gets crushed is hard to recover, which is why a large κ makes inverse problems and training unstable. The classic rule from numerical analysis is that solving a system with W can lose about log₁₀ κ significant digits. float32 has a machine epsilon of about 1.19 × 10⁻⁷, roughly seven digits. So κ = 10⁴ leaves about three. Singular values come back later as the per-layer gain in the vanishing-gradient slide.
