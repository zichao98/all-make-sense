Engineering Math Sense · 12　　Natural log. Curves: −ln p for p from 0 to 1; the dot marks each case.

Cross-entropy for one example is minus the natural log of the probability the model gave to the correct class. It is a measure of surprise. A confident correct prediction, p = 0.9, costs 0.105. A coin flip, p = 0.5, costs 0.693. A confident wrong prediction, p = 0.01, costs 4.61, more than forty times the first case, which is why a few confidently wrong examples can dominate the average loss. The most useful sanity check follows directly. Before training, a reasonable model spreads probability roughly evenly over K classes, so p is about 1/K and the loss is about ln K: 0.693 for 2 classes, 2.303 for 10, 6.908 for 1,000. If your first loss is much higher than ln K, the initial outputs are already overconfident, and that is worth fixing before tuning anything else.
