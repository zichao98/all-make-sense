Part One

Three translations

Reality has to become numbers first.

Images, sound, temperature, risk and behaviour all pass through the same three steps before a computer can work on them.

The article points out that the real world cannot be handed to a computer directly. Before any algorithm runs, the problem is translated three times: continuous quantities are discretized, objects and their relations are arranged as vectors, matrices and tensors, and the answer is found by iteration from a starting guess. This part takes those three steps one at a time.
