Engineering Math Sense · 13

Computers do not have real numbers; they have floats with a fixed range and precision. The largest float32 is about 3.4 × 10³⁸, and its natural log is 88.72, so e to the z overflows to infinity once z passes about 88.7. In float16 the largest value is 65,504, so the limit is z ≈ 11.09, which ordinary logits easily exceed. That is why mixed-precision training keeps sensitive operations in float32 and uses loss scaling. Precision runs out too: float32 has a machine epsilon of 1.19 × 10⁻⁷, so 1 + 10⁻⁸ is exactly 1. The fix for softmax is to subtract the largest logit before exponentiating. Dividing numerator and denominator by the same e to the m leaves the result unchanged, but every exponent is now at most zero. For logits 1000, 1001, 1002, the naive formula computes infinity over infinity and returns NaN. The shifted formula returns 0.090, 0.245 and 0.665. These values were checked in NumPy.
