Engineering Math Sense · 16　　Operation counts for a full circular convolution of length 4,096, constant factors ignored.

Fourier analysis rewrites a signal as a sum of sines. Two facts make it useful. First, the convolution theorem: convolving two signals is the same as multiplying their spectra. Filtering becomes a mask on frequencies. Second, structure and noise look different there. The animation builds a clean signal from 3 and 7 cycles, adds Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.45, and computes the discrete Fourier transform of 128 samples directly in the browser. The two tones stand out as two tall bars, and the noise is spread thinly over all 64 frequencies. Keeping only frequencies up to 8 cuts the error against the clean signal from 0.41 to 0.20. The FFT computes a transform in about n log₂ n operations instead of n². For a full circular convolution of length 4,096 that is about 16.8 million multiplications directly versus about 150,000 through three FFTs and a pointwise product, roughly 110 times fewer, ignoring constant factors. That is why large convolutions and spectral methods go through the FFT.
